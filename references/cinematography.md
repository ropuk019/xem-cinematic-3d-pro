# Cinematography

## Shot grammar
- Establishing shot: communicate environment and scale.
- Hero shot: strongest silhouette and clearest product read.
- Detail shot: material, craft, interface, or feature.
- Transition shot: motivated movement linking visual beats.
- End card: stable composition, exact supplied copy, adequate hold.

## Composition
Use thirds, symmetry, leading lines, negative space, and foreground/background separation deliberately. A centered product can feel premium when the environment is balanced. Reserve safe margins for platform overlays. In vertical video, avoid placing critical copy at the extreme top or bottom.

## Lens language
- 24–35 mm equivalent: energetic, spatial, more perspective.
- 40–60 mm: neutral product presentation.
- 75–100 mm: compressed, premium detail framing.
These are visual starting points; the Three.js camera uses focal length/FOV relationships rather than a physical lens by default.

## Movement
Prefer one motivated camera move per shot. Dolly reveals depth; orbit reveals form; tilt reveals height; crane changes scale. Use ease-in/ease-out and intentional holds. Constant rotation often looks like a product viewer, not a directed commercial.
